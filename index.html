Welcome.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Virginia Tech. My research is in microeconomic theory, particularly networks and game theory.

The decisions of individuals and firms often depend on the actions of others, and these interactions are shaped by networks. Yet individuals rarely know the full structure of the networks in which they interact. My research examines how this limited knowledge of the network structure shapes strategic behavior and economic outcomes. I also study its implications for the design of effective policies. For example, in my Job Market Paper, I examine how a policymaker should target individuals in a network when those individuals have limited information.

At Virginia Tech, I have taught a variety of courses, including undergraduate Principles of Microeconomics, Intermediate Microeconomics, and Math Camp for incoming PhD students. My teaching emphasizes connecting economic theory with real-world applications. As part of this approach, I co-developed interactive online games for my Principles course in which students learn economics by making decisions in a fictional town (Marginal Falls Economic Quests).

I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market.
