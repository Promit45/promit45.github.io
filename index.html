Welcome.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Virginia Tech. My research is in microeconomic theory, particularly networks and game theory.

I study how people make decisions when they know their own connections but have limited information about the broader network. I examine how their beliefs shape behavior, what they learn from repeated interactions, and how limited information affects policy interventions.

In my Job Market Paper, I study whom a policymaker should remove from a network when only the removed individual’s neighbors observe the removal. Who knows about the removal can change whom the policymaker should target.

At Virginia Tech, I have taught undergraduate Principles of Microeconomics and Microeconomic Theory, as well as Math Camp for incoming PhD students. I received the department’s Graduate Student Instructor of the Year award in 2025. For my Principles course, I co-developed Marginal Falls Economic Quests, a collection of interactive online games that students play together in class. Each game places students in the fictional town of Marginal Falls where they make economic choices, and the class then discusses the economics behind those choices.

I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market.
