Optimal Targeting and Information Spillovers in Networks

Whom should a policymaker target when an intervention is observed only locally?

Read paper
Abstract

We study optimal targeting in networks where agents know only their direct connections and removals are locally observed. A fully informed planner chooses a player to remove, thereby determining both which interactions are disrupted and who becomes informed. We show that the effect of removal decomposes into the direct loss of the targeted player’s activity and information spillovers shaped by agents’ beliefs about who else observes the removal. These spillovers can overturn complete-information targeting predictions, making a less-connected player a more effective target than a more influential one. We also identify conditions under which optimal targeting depends on the degrees of the target and her neighbors.

Competitive Polarization on Social Media Platforms

With B. Pochko and J. Park
